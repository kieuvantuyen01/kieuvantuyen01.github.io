Biography & Overview

About Me & Academic Journey

Bridging mathematical rigor, Boolean Satisfiability solvers, and AI-driven reasoning to solve complex industrial optimization problems.

I recently graduated with a Master of Science in Computer Science (December 2025) from VNU University of Engineering and Technology (VNU-UET), achieving a GPA of 3.92 / 4.00. My Master's thesis, titled "Optimal SAT Encoding Methods for Combinatorial Optimization and Scheduling Problems", was awarded an outstanding score of 9.8 / 10.0 under the guidance of Dr. To Van Khanh at the Laboratory of Embedded Systems.

Prior to my M.Sc., I received my Bachelor of Science in Computer Science from VNU-UET with a GPA of 3.83 / 4.00, where my thesis on "Verification of Multithreaded Programs based on Symbolic Execution" (defended with 9.5/10) earned the Council's Excellent Thesis Honor.

My research lies at the intersection of Combinatorial Optimization, Boolean Satisfiability (SAT / MaxSAT), Constraint Programming (CP), and Artificial Intelligence. Specifically, I investigate how compact clause representations, symmetry breaking, and dynamic constraint learning can scale exact solvers to problems previously deemed intractable for commercial MIP engines like IBM CPLEX, Gurobi, and Google OR-Tools.

I am strongly interested in pursuing PhD research to explore how Large Language Models (LLMs) and neuro-symbolic techniques can automatically formulate mathematical optimization models from natural language, synthesize efficient SAT/MIP encodings, and dynamically configure solvers for real-world logistics, train rescheduling, and power management systems.

A Compact SAT Encoding for Non-Preemptive Task Scheduling on Multiple Identical Resources

T. V. Kieu, T. V. Khanh

Cybernetics and Information Technologies (CIT), Vol. 25, No. 3, pp. 104–122, 2025.

Scholar
Abstract

This paper addresses the non-preemptive task scheduling problem on multiple identical resources, an NP-hard optimization challenge widely found in manufacturing and high-performance computing. We present a novel compact Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) encoding scheme that significantly reduces the number of clauses and auxiliary variables while preserving strong unit propagation. Experimental evaluations against leading commercial solvers show that our SAT model outperforms IBM CPLEX and Google OR-Tools on difficult benchmark instances.

Sequential Counter Encoding for Staircase At-Most-One Constraints

H. X. Truong, T. V. Kieu, T. V. Khanh

Proceedings of the 17th International Conference on Agents and Artificial Intelligence (ICAART), Vol. 2, pp. 164–175, SCITEPRESS, 2025.

Scholar
Abstract

At-Most-One (AMO) constraints frequently appear in combinatorial optimization and planning. When consecutive sequences of constraints overlap in staircase topologies, direct pairwise encodings produce excessive clauses. We propose an innovative Sequential Counter encoding specifically tailored for Staircase AMO constraints that reduces clause counts by up to 40% while preserving unit propagation strength.
